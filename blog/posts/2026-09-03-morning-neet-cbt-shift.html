NEET-UG Goes Digital: A New Era for Medical Entrance?

September 03, 2026 · 3 min read

The National Testing Agency (NTA) recently announced a significant change for the NEET-UG medical entrance examination: starting from 2027, the exam will transition from its traditional pen-and-paper format to computer-based testing (CBT). This decision, which follows recent controversies surrounding paper leaks and irregularities in the traditional exam, aims to modernise the assessment process, enhance security, and ensure greater transparency. The shift impacts over 2.2 million aspirants annually, sparking widespread debate about its potential benefits and challenges for India's future doctors.

Why people say yes

Advocates for CBT highlight its potential to significantly enhance examination security. Digital exams reduce reliance on physical question papers and their transportation, thereby minimising the risk of leaks that have plagued previous examinations. This move is seen as a crucial step to restore integrity and trust in the system. Furthermore, computer-based testing offers a robust digital audit trail and can integrate advanced biometric features, making impersonation more difficult and increasing overall transparency for all stakeholders.

Another key advantage is the promise of faster and more accurate results. With responses recorded electronically, the evaluation process can be expedited, eliminating errors associated with manual OMR sheet processing, such as incorrect filling or scanning issues. This efficiency can lead to quicker admission processes and reduce the waiting period for anxious students. Additionally, CBT formats often allow for easier navigation between questions, the ability to mark questions for review, and on-screen timers, potentially improving the candidate experience.

CBT also brings NEET-UG in line with other major national entrance examinations like JEE, which are already conducted in a computer-based mode. This consistency can standardise testing practices across the country and leverage existing infrastructure. Moreover, in cases of localised technical glitches, re-tests can be confined to affected centres or shifts, avoiding nationwide cancellations and distress to a large number of students. The environmental benefit of significantly reducing paper consumption is also a positive aspect.

Why people say no

Critics express significant concerns about the digital divide, particularly for students from rural and underprivileged backgrounds. Many aspirants, especially those from remote areas, may have limited or no prior experience with computers or stable internet access. This lack of familiarity could put them at a disadvantage, irrespective of their academic capabilities, and potentially exclude deserving candidates. The shift risks replacing one set of problems with another, where access to technology becomes a new barrier to entry.

Another major apprehension revolves around the reliability of technology. Technical glitches, software failures, or internet connectivity issues during the examination could severely disrupt the test-taking experience and unfairly affect students' performance. While proponents suggest isolated re-tests, even a small technical error can cause immense stress and negatively impact a student's score in such a high-stakes exam. Implementing a large-scale CBT infrastructure across thousands of centres reliably will be a complex and massive undertaking for the NTA.

The move to multiple shifts, necessary to accommodate the vast number of candidates in a CBT format, introduces the challenge of score normalisation. Ensuring absolute fairness and parity in difficulty levels across different question papers and sessions is extremely difficult. Even small differences in paper difficulty, if not adequately normalised, can lead to significant rank swings in a highly competitive exam like NEET. Furthermore, some experts argue that simply changing the delivery mechanism to CBT does not address the fundamental issues of exam integrity, as instances of irregularities have been reported even in computer-based formats, and the question-setting process itself needs stronger monitoring.

Key facts

  • The National Testing Agency (NTA) announced that NEET-UG will transition to a Computer-Based Test (CBT) format starting from the 2027 examination cycle.
  • This shift is primarily aimed at enhancing exam security, preventing paper leaks, and improving the overall transparency and efficiency of the examination process.
  • The CBT format for NEET-UG is expected to be conducted across multiple sessions over several days to accommodate approximately 25 lakh candidates, necessitating a score normalisation process.

The bottom line

The transition of NEET-UG to computer-based testing represents a pivotal moment in India's medical education landscape. While promising enhanced security, efficiency, and alignment with modern testing practices, it simultaneously presents significant challenges related to digital equity, technical stability, and fair score normalisation. The success of this reform will largely depend on meticulous planning, robust infrastructure development, and proactive measures to ensure equitable access and a smooth, stress-free transition for all aspiring medical students across the nation.
