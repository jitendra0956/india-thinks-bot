Board Marks vs. Entrance Exams: Reshaping India's University Admissions

August 09, 2026 · 3 min read

Nationwide student protests following the NEET paper leaks have brought India's high-stakes entrance examination system under intense scrutiny. The recent controversies have reignited a crucial debate: should university admissions prioritize the consistent academic performance reflected in board examination marks, or should the focus remain on the competitive aptitude measured by entrance exams? This question holds significant implications for millions of students and the future of higher education in the country.

Why people say yes

Advocates for prioritizing board marks argue that these scores offer a more holistic and reliable assessment of a student's academic journey over two crucial years. Unlike a single high-pressure entrance exam, board marks reflect sustained effort, understanding, and performance across multiple subjects, providing a broader picture of a student's capabilities.

A major concern with entrance exams is the booming and often expensive coaching industry, which creates an unequal playing field. Shifting focus to board marks could reduce the perceived necessity of external coaching, allowing students from diverse socio-economic backgrounds a fairer chance at higher education by valuing their school-based learning.

The "one-shot" nature of many entrance exams places immense pressure on students, leading to significant mental stress and, in tragic cases, suicides. Emphasizing board marks could alleviate this intense, singular pressure, promoting a more balanced approach to learning and reducing the psychological burden on young aspirants.

Why people say no

Opponents of prioritizing board marks point to the vast differences in marking standards and curricula across India's numerous school boards. Entrance exams, like NEET and CUET, provide a standardized, common yardstick to compare students from varied educational backgrounds, ensuring a level playing field for admissions to national institutions.

Entrance exams are designed to assess analytical ability, problem-solving skills, and in-depth conceptual understanding, rather than just rote memorization often associated with board exams. This helps universities identify students with the specific aptitudes required for demanding higher education courses, potentially leading to better academic outcomes and lower dropout rates.

Given the exceptionally high number of applicants for limited university seats, entrance exams serve as an efficient and competitive mechanism to filter candidates. For instance, around 1.4 million students registered for NEET 2024 for less than 100,000 MBBS seats. This system helps manage massive applicant pools effectively, though controversies like paper leaks highlight the need for stronger integrity.

Key facts

  • The NEET-UG 2026 paper leak led to nationwide student protests, demanding accountability and resulting in the resignation of the Union Education Minister.
  • A government panel is currently considering a proposal to give school board performance "substantial weightage – up to 50%, suitably normalised – in admission/merit" for professional and undergraduate programs.
  • Many competitive exams, such as JEE Advanced and NEET-UG, already require minimum Class 12 board marks as an eligibility criterion (e.g., 75% for JEE Advanced or top 20 percentile, 50% for NEET general category).

The bottom line

The debate over prioritizing board marks versus entrance exams for university admissions is complex, with valid arguments on both sides. The recent protests have underscored the urgent need for reforms to ensure fairness, transparency, and reduced stress in the admissions process. Finding a balanced approach that leverages the strengths of both assessment methods—perhaps through a composite score or re-evaluation of exam design—could be key to building a more equitable and effective higher education gateway in India.
