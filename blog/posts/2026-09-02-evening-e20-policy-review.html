India's E20 Policy: Balancing Fuel Security with Food and Consumer Costs

September 02, 2026 · 3 min read

India's ambitious E20 petrol policy, which blends 20% ethanol with gasoline, has recently come under scrutiny. On August 21, 2026, the Congress party demanded an immediate review of the policy, citing growing concerns over its impact on essential food prices and reported reductions in vehicle mileage. This call for a reassessment brings the 'food versus fuel' debate sharply into focus, prompting a re-evaluation of the policy's broader economic and social implications for the nation.

Why people say yes

Advocates for reviewing the E20 policy primarily highlight its perceived negative impact on household budgets. A major concern is the diversion of food grains like sugarcane, maize, and rice for ethanol production, which critics argue contributes to rising food prices. Reports indicate significant increases in the prices of staples such as sugar and eggs, with sugar prices rising by 40% in three months and egg prices by 35-40% in a year, partly attributed to maize diversion impacting poultry feed costs. This diversion, critics contend, could threaten food and nutrition security, particularly for vulnerable populations, and increase reliance on imported food items.

Another significant point of contention is the reported reduction in vehicle mileage. Government assessments indicate that E20 petrol can decrease fuel efficiency by 2-6%, depending on the vehicle type and age. However, some independent tests and consumer surveys have reported even larger drops, with some owners of older vehicles experiencing mileage losses of over 10%. Since E20 is sold at similar prices to conventional petrol, consumers effectively pay more per kilometre, increasing transport costs. Additionally, concerns linger regarding the long-term compatibility and potential wear and tear on vehicles not specifically designed for E20, especially older models.

Critics also argue that the policy reduces consumer choice, as non-ethanol petrol options become scarce. The rapid expansion of grain-based ethanol production has also raised questions about agricultural land use and water consumption, particularly for water-intensive crops like sugarcane and rice. They suggest that while energy security is vital, it should not come at the cost of compromising food affordability, agricultural sustainability, or the economic well-being of ordinary citizens.

Why people say no

Supporters of the E20 policy emphasize its critical role in enhancing India's energy security. As India imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements, domestic ethanol blending significantly reduces this dependence, shielding the nation from volatile global oil prices and geopolitical disruptions. This strategic move is projected to save the country substantial foreign exchange, with annual savings estimated at around ₹43,000 crore at 20% blending.

The policy also provides a substantial boost to the rural economy and farmer incomes. By creating an additional and assured market for agricultural produce like sugarcane and maize, ethanol production offers farmers a stable revenue stream and better price realization for their crops. This diversification helps sugar mills and distilleries, contributing to the overall stability of the agricultural sector.

Furthermore, the E20 policy aligns with India's environmental goals. Ethanol-blended fuels are considered cleaner-burning, leading to reduced greenhouse gas emissions, including carbon monoxide and hydrocarbons. This contributes to improved air quality and helps India meet its climate commitments. The government also asserts that E20 is a safe and thoroughly tested fuel, with most new vehicles launched from 2023 being E20-compliant, and no widespread engine failures reported.

Key facts

  • Congress demanded a review of the E20 policy on August 21, 2026, citing impacts on food prices and vehicle mileage.
  • E20 petrol can reduce vehicle fuel efficiency by 2-6%, with some surveys reporting over 10% loss for older vehicles.
  • Diversion of crops for ethanol has been linked to rising prices of essential food items like sugar and eggs.
  • The E20 policy is aimed at reducing India's crude oil import dependence and saving foreign exchange.
  • It also supports farmer incomes by creating an alternative market for agricultural produce.

The bottom line

The debate surrounding India's E20 petrol policy underscores a complex challenge: balancing national energy security with domestic food security and consumer welfare. While the policy offers strategic advantages in reducing oil imports and supporting the agricultural sector, concerns about food price inflation and vehicle performance necessitate careful consideration. Any path forward will require a balanced approach that addresses both national objectives and the daily realities faced by Indian citizens, potentially through transparent assessments and adaptive policy measures.
