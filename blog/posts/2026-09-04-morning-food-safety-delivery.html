Online Bites, Real Risks: Should Food Delivery Face Stricter Safety Rules?

September 04, 2026 · 3 min read

Recent viral incidents in Bengaluru, where customers reported finding foreign objects like worms in their online food orders and partially eaten meals, have cast a spotlight on critical food safety lapses within the burgeoning online delivery sector. These alarming events, coupled with intensified inspections by food safety authorities across India, underscore a growing concern: are current regulations sufficient to ensure the safety and hygiene of food delivered to our doorsteps, or is it time for stricter enforcement?

Why people say yes

The push for stricter food safety regulations largely stems from a desire to protect public health and enhance consumer trust. Viral incidents, such as the discovery of worms in a Bengaluru salad and expired products from quick-commerce platforms, highlight potential severe health risks including food poisoning and infections. Enhanced regulations could enforce mandatory quality checks at every stage, from kitchen preparation to final delivery, ensuring that consumers receive safe and hygienic food.

Furthermore, stricter rules would compel greater accountability from all stakeholders in the online food delivery ecosystem. The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has already mandated that e-commerce platforms display food labels and expiry dates, and holds them accountable for the food sold. More stringent regulations could solidify this responsibility, ensuring platforms and their listed vendors adhere to uniform, high standards, thereby reducing incidents of contamination, adulteration, and misleading practices.

The rapid expansion of India's online food delivery market, now a multi-billion dollar industry, necessitates a robust regulatory framework that can keep pace with its growth. With millions relying on these services daily, the sheer volume of transactions increases the potential for safety lapses. Stricter regulations are seen as essential to proactively address these systemic challenges, prevent future outbreaks, and build long-term consumer confidence in a sector that has become integral to urban life.

Why people say no

Conversely, arguments against stricter regulations often center on the potential economic burden on businesses, particularly small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and cloud kitchens. Implementing new, more stringent hygiene and safety protocols, such as frequent audits, advanced temperature control systems, and comprehensive staff training, could entail significant costs. These increased operational expenses might disproportionately affect smaller vendors, potentially leading to higher food prices for consumers or even business closures.

Another concern is the practicality and logistical complexity of enforcing overly strict regulations across a vast and diverse country like India. Monitoring thousands of cloud kitchens, restaurants, and delivery personnel, each with varying operational capacities and locations, presents a formidable challenge for regulatory bodies like FSSAI. Critics argue that current enforcement mechanisms, if adequately deployed, are sufficient, and the focus should be on strengthening existing oversight rather than introducing new, potentially unmanageable rules.

Moreover, some argue that the existing FSSAI framework already provides a comprehensive set of guidelines for food safety, requiring mandatory licensing, hygiene standards, and labeling norms for all food business operators, including online aggregators and cloud kitchens. The issue, they contend, lies not with the regulations themselves but with inconsistent compliance and enforcement. Implementing yet another layer of "stricter" rules without first ensuring effective implementation of current ones could lead to regulatory fatigue and further complications.

Key facts

  • The FSSAI has intensified its enforcement efforts, conducting over 24,000 raids across India since April 2026, targeting hygiene violations in various food establishments, including quick-commerce warehouses.
  • Consumer complaints against online food delivery apps are significant, with FSSAI registering over 21,000 grievances in the last five years related to issues like contaminated food and misleading claims.
  • Online food delivery platforms are legally mandated to obtain FSSAI licenses, list only FSSAI-registered businesses, and ensure product listings display accurate information and have adequate shelf life.

The bottom line

The debate surrounding stricter food safety regulations for online deliveries highlights a critical tension between consumer protection and business feasibility. While recent incidents underscore the urgent need to safeguard public health and rebuild trust, concerns remain about the practical implications and economic impact on a rapidly expanding industry. Finding a balanced approach that enhances oversight without stifling innovation or overburdening businesses will be crucial for the sustainable growth of India's online food delivery sector.
