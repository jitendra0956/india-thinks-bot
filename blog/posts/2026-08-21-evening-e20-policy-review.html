E20 Fuel Policy: Balancing Energy Security and Food Prices in India

August 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Congress on August 21, 2026, called for an immediate review of India's E20 ethanol blending policy, citing concerns over rising food prices and increased fuel expenses for consumers. This demand ignites a crucial debate about the balance between energy independence and food security, a policy designed to reduce reliance on imported crude oil while facing criticism for its potential impact on household budgets and agricultural priorities.

Why people say yes

Critics argue that diverting food grains like maize and rice, along with sugarcane, towards ethanol production directly impacts the availability and cost of essential food items. Reports suggest a rise in prices for sugar, jaggery, rice, and maize flour, placing a significant burden on consumers' kitchens.

Many vehicle owners report a noticeable drop in mileage when using E20 fuel due to ethanol's lower energy content compared to petrol. Furthermore, concerns are high regarding the compatibility of older vehicles with higher ethanol blends, potentially leading to increased maintenance costs and even engine damage.

The aggressive push for ethanol production from grains has led to a shift in agricultural land use, with farmers increasingly favoring crops like maize for ethanol over pulses and oilseeds. This change in cropping patterns raises long-term worries about India's food security, potential dependence on edible oil imports, and volatility in domestic food prices.

Why people say no

A primary objective of the E20 policy is to reduce India's heavy reliance on imported crude oil, which accounts for approximately 85% of its oil needs. By blending domestically produced ethanol, the policy aims to shield the nation from volatile global oil prices and strengthen its energy independence.

The ethanol blending program creates a stable and expanding market for agricultural feedstocks like sugarcane and maize. This increased demand provides farmers with an additional and predictable income source, contributing significantly to rural economic growth and stability.

E20 fuel is touted as a cleaner alternative to conventional petrol, leading to a reduction in greenhouse gas emissions and improved air quality. The government highlights that ethanol blending has already prevented millions of tonnes of CO2 emissions, aligning with India's climate change mitigation goals.

Key facts

  • India achieved its 20% ethanol blending target in 2025, five years ahead of schedule.
  • The area of land linked to ethanol feedstocks has increased more than eight-fold in four years, from 0.7 million hectares in 2020-21 to 5.7 million hectares in 2024-25.
  • The government states the EBP program saved over ₹1.90 lakh crore in foreign exchange between 2014-15 and May 2026.

The bottom line

The debate surrounding India's E20 policy highlights a complex interplay of national priorities. While proponents emphasize its role in bolstering energy security, supporting farmers, and promoting environmental sustainability, critics raise valid concerns about its potential consequences on food prices, vehicle performance, and overall food security. As India navigates its energy transition, a careful evaluation of these trade-offs remains essential to ensure an inclusive and sustainable path forward.
