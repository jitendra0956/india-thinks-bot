AI's Impact on India: Threat or Opportunity for Jobs?

September 12, 2026 · 3 min read

A recent discussion on September 9, 2026, brought to light projections indicating that 400,000 to 500,000 Indian jobs are at risk due to the advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and automation in the coming years, particularly affecting mid-level professionals and those in routine roles. This alarming forecast has ignited a critical debate across the nation: Does AI pose a significant threat to India's burgeoning workforce, or does it herald a new era of economic opportunity and growth?

Why people say yes

Concerns about AI's impact on Indian jobs are substantial, primarily driven by the increasing automation of routine tasks. Sectors like IT, finance, and customer services are particularly vulnerable, with forecasts suggesting that AI automation could displace 68% of white-collar jobs in India within the next five years. This includes roles in manual testing, data entry, and basic coding. The NITI Aayog estimates that without large-scale reskilling, technology-based jobs could decline from 7.5-8 million in 2023 to 6 million by 2031, with customer experience jobs also seeing a reduction.

Another significant worry is the declining demand for entry-level workers. Reports indicate that 55% of IT firms have seen a decrease in entry-level hiring, as AI takes over tasks traditionally performed by junior employees. This creates a skills mismatch, leaving a portion of the workforce vulnerable if they cannot adapt to new technological requirements. Furthermore, the informal sector, which employs a large percentage of India's workforce, has limited access to formal training and upskilling infrastructure, exacerbating the risk of displacement for millions. The World Economic Forum suggests that AI-driven automation could displace up to 23% of India's jobs by 2030, impacting over 100 million workers.

The sheer scale of India's workforce and its position as a global IT and back-office hub make it particularly susceptible to AI-driven disruption. Many fear that while AI creates new, highly specialized roles, the transition for displaced workers into these new positions will be challenging due to the significant difference in skill sets required. This could lead to a widening gap between those with AI-ready skills and those without, potentially increasing unemployment in traditional sectors.

Why people say no

Conversely, many experts argue that AI is a net job creator and a powerful engine for economic growth in India. While some jobs may be displaced, AI is simultaneously generating new roles in areas such as AI development, data science, cybersecurity, and AI ethics. A Nomura report from August 2026 indicated that AI-related hiring in India (83,100 new jobs) outpaced AI-related layoffs and attrition (31,921 jobs) between 2022 and August 2026, suggesting a net positive impact on employment so far. PwC also projected that AI could add 9 million new jobs in India by 2035.

Beyond job creation, AI is expected to significantly boost India's economy through enhanced productivity and efficiency. NASSCOM estimates that AI will contribute a substantial $967 billion to the Indian economy by 2035. A McKinsey Global Institute report further suggests that AI adoption could add an estimated $957 billion to India's GDP by 2035 and IBM research projects AI could unlock over $500 billion in economic value for India by 2030. This growth stems from AI's ability to automate tasks, optimize processes, and improve decision-making across various sectors like manufacturing, agriculture, healthcare, and finance.

The Indian government and industry are actively working to equip the workforce with AI skills. Initiatives like the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) 4.0, which offers AI-based training, and the 'YUVAi - Youth for Unnati and Vikas with AI' program, aim to build digital readiness from the school level. The IndiaAI Mission, launched in March 2024 with a significant outlay, aims to position India as a global AI leader by 2030 by democratizing AI access and promoting its adoption. India also has a strong foundation of AI-native talent, with over 90% of its early-career technology talent being AI-native or AI-proficient by 2026. These efforts highlight a proactive approach to reskilling and upskilling, preparing the workforce for the demands of an AI-driven economy.

Key facts

  • A September 9, 2026, discussion projected 400,000-500,000 Indian jobs at risk due to AI and automation in the coming years, particularly in routine and mid-level roles.
  • AI is estimated to contribute $967 billion to the Indian economy by 2035, significantly boosting GDP.
  • Over 90% of India's early-career technology talent is considered AI-native or AI-proficient as of 2026, indicating a strong foundation for future growth.

The bottom line

The debate surrounding AI's impact on Indian jobs is complex, presenting both palpable threats and transformative opportunities. While automation undeniably poses a risk of displacement for certain job categories, particularly routine and entry-level roles, the concurrent creation of new, specialized AI-driven positions and the potential for substantial economic growth offer a contrasting perspective. India's ability to navigate this technological shift successfully will largely depend on widespread reskilling initiatives, strategic investments in AI infrastructure, and fostering an adaptable workforce ready for the evolving demands of the future.
