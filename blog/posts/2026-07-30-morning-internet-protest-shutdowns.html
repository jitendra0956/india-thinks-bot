India's Digital Dilemma: Justifying Internet Shutdowns During Protests

July 30, 2026 · 3 min read

On July 23, 2026, India's Ministry of Home Affairs ordered a temporary suspension of mobile internet services for eight hours at the site of youth-led protests in New Delhi. This decision reignites a persistent and often contentious debate across the nation: are internet shutdowns during periods of public unrest a justifiable measure? The incident in the capital highlights the ongoing tension between maintaining public order and upholding citizens' digital rights, prompting a closer look at the arguments surrounding such governmental actions.

Why people say yes

Proponents argue internet shutdowns are crucial for law enforcement to de-escalate volatile situations. They contend social media can spread misinformation, incite violence, and coordinate unlawful assemblies, escalating peaceful protests into riots. Temporarily blocking internet access can disrupt harmful communications, preventing widespread disorder and protecting property.

Authorities also cite national security and public safety. Restricting communication channels can offer a tactical advantage in crowd control and prevent hostile elements from organizing attacks. The internet's speed allows quick mobilization, so a temporary shutdown provides critical time for security agencies to respond effectively.

Supporters emphasize these measures are often temporary and localized, addressing specific, immediate threats. They are presented as a necessary evil to restore normalcy and prevent larger disruptions or loss of life. This perspective frames shutdowns as a proactive step to prevent worse outcomes.

Why people say no

Conversely, critics view internet shutdowns as a disproportionate measure infringing fundamental human rights. They argue such actions violate freedom of speech and expression, cornerstones of democracy, preventing citizens from accessing vital information or expressing grievances peacefully.

The economic impact is another significant concern. Modern economies rely heavily on digital connectivity. A shutdown, even brief, can cause substantial financial losses for individuals, businesses, and the wider economy, disrupting essential services like banking, education, and healthcare.

Opponents also highlight the lack of transparency. They argue shutdowns stifle journalistic reporting, prevent human rights observers from documenting events, and create information vacuums fostering distrust. Many believe less restrictive measures should be explored first.

Key facts

  • India has consistently led the world in the number of internet shutdowns imposed, with 65 shutdowns recorded in 2025 and 920 total since 2016, making it the country with the highest historical tally.
  • Internet shutdowns in India are primarily governed by Section 20 of the Telecommunications Act, 2023, and the Telecommunications (Temporary Suspension of Services) Rules, 2024.
  • These rules empower the Union Home Secretary or a State Home Secretary to issue orders for internet suspension, citing reasons of public emergency or public safety, which must be reasoned, published, and reviewed.

The bottom line

The debate over internet shutdowns during protests underscores a complex dilemma: balancing state responsibilities for public order and safety with citizens' fundamental rights to freedom of expression and access to information. While authorities often cite security concerns and the prevention of violence, critics point to the profound implications for human rights, economic activity, and democratic principles. As digital connectivity becomes increasingly integral to daily life, the justification and implementation of such measures will continue to be a crucial point of discussion in India's evolving digital landscape.
