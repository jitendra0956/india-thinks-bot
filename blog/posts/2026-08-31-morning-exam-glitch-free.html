NEET PG Glitches: Who's Accountable for Flawless Exams?

August 31, 2026 · 3 min read

The recent announcement of a NEET PG 2026 re-exam on September 5, following significant power and technical glitches at Jaipur centres on August 27, 2026, has once again brought the reliability of India's examination system into sharp focus. This incident, where candidates reported repeated system shutdowns and delayed starts, highlights a persistent challenge in conducting large-scale computer-based tests and raises critical questions about accountability and fairness. Such disruptions not only cause immense stress for aspirants but also cast a shadow over the integrity of the entire examination process, impacting thousands who invest years in preparation.

Why people say yes

Many argue that exam bodies must ensure glitch-free testing environments to uphold the fundamental principle of fair evaluation. Candidates invest significant time and money in preparing for crucial exams like NEET PG, and technical failures can unfairly disadvantage them, irrespective of their preparation. A reliable testing environment ensures that every candidate has an equal opportunity to perform, preventing scenarios where technical issues, such as systems shutting down multiple times or questions appearing incorrectly, compromise a student's ability to complete the test. Moreover, guaranteeing a seamless experience builds trust in the examination system, assuring students and parents that the results accurately reflect merit rather than being influenced by preventable technical shortcomings.

The integrity of national examinations is paramount for maintaining public confidence in the education and recruitment sectors. Technical glitches can lead to concerns about data confidentiality and the fairness of the examination, as seen in the Jaipur incident where candidates questioned the security of questions appearing on screens before systems failed. Ensuring a robust and glitch-free environment acts as a safeguard against such integrity breaches, reinforcing the credibility of the entire process. Furthermore, preventing technical disruptions directly contributes to student well-being by alleviating the considerable mental stress and anxiety associated with exam day uncertainty. The emotional toll of disruptions can be profound, impacting performance and potentially affecting future prospects for those who have dedicated years to their studies.

Why people say no

Conversely, some argue that achieving a completely glitch-free testing environment is an incredibly complex and often unpredictable undertaking, especially at the massive scale of Indian competitive exams. Technical failures, such as power outages or server issues, can occur unexpectedly despite extensive preparations. Even with advanced infrastructure, unforeseen bugs or network instability, particularly in diverse geographical locations with varying internet connectivity, pose significant challenges. The sheer volume of candidates and testing centres across the country makes it challenging to guarantee absolute perfection in every single instance.

The financial implications of establishing and maintaining an infrastructure that guarantees zero glitches are substantial. Implementing redundant systems, high-speed internet across all centres, and cutting-edge cybersecurity measures requires immense investment. While reforms are underway, including developing secure testing centres with uninterrupted power and CCTV monitoring, the cost of perfection could be prohibitive and potentially divert resources from other critical areas of exam administration, such as content development or accessibility initiatives. Prioritizing the core academic aspects of an exam, such as the quality of question papers and the fairness of the syllabus, might be seen as equally, if not more, important than absolute technical infallibility, given resource constraints.

Key facts

  • Technical disruptions, including repeated computer shutdowns and delayed exam starts, led to protests by candidates during the NEET PG 2026 examination in Jaipur.
  • The Indian government has constituted a High-Powered Task Force on examination reforms to make the system more transparent, leak-proof, and technology-driven, including measures for strengthening infrastructure and deploying appropriate technologies.
  • Challenges in conducting large-scale online exams in India include internet connectivity issues in rural areas, the need for robust question banks, effective communication with candidates, and ensuring online exam security.

The bottom line

The debate over whether exam bodies should guarantee glitch-free testing environments underscores the tension between aspirational ideals of perfect fairness and the practical realities of large-scale digital operations. While the imperative to provide a smooth and equitable experience for every candidate is strong, the inherent complexities and costs associated with achieving absolute technical infallibility present formidable obstacles. Moving forward, a balanced approach that continuously invests in robust technology and infrastructure, while also implementing comprehensive contingency plans and transparent grievance redressal mechanisms, will be crucial to strengthening public trust and ensuring the integrity of India's vital examination system.
