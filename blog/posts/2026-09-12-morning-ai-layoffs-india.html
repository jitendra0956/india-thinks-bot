AI vs. Jobs: The Debate Over Layoffs in India's Tech Sector

September 12, 2026 · 3 min read

The global tech landscape is undergoing a significant transformation, marked by a recent wave of job cuts. Over 6,300 tech jobs were eliminated globally in the first 10 days of September 2026, with major companies like Uber and PayPal announcing reductions impacting their Indian operations, partly attributing these changes to an increased focus on Artificial Intelligence (AI). This trend has ignited a fierce debate in India: are AI-driven layoffs justifiable?

Why people say yes

Proponents argue that AI-driven layoffs are a necessary step for companies to remain competitive and efficient in a rapidly evolving global market. AI technologies significantly boost productivity and reduce operational costs by automating repetitive tasks, allowing businesses to streamline operations and focus resources on strategic initiatives. This efficiency is crucial for Indian tech companies to compete on a global scale, attracting investments and fostering economic growth.

Furthermore, the adoption of AI is seen as a catalyst for innovation. While some jobs are displaced, new high-skill roles emerge in areas such as AI engineering, data analytics, and cybersecurity, demanding a more specialized workforce. Companies can reallocate talent and capital towards advanced technologies, driving further technological advancements and maintaining India's position as a global tech hub. This strategic pivot is viewed as essential for long-term resilience and growth in the digital economy.

By embracing AI and making tough workforce decisions, companies aim to create leaner, more agile organizations. This shift is not just about cost-cutting but about a fundamental restructuring to leverage AI's potential for superior outcomes, allowing for faster decision-making and improved quality across various functions, from manufacturing to financial services. Ultimately, for many, these layoffs represent a painful but unavoidable step towards a more technologically advanced and globally competitive Indian tech industry.

Why people say no

Critics, however, contend that AI-driven layoffs create widespread job insecurity and have severe socio-economic consequences. The displacement disproportionately affects middle and low-skilled workers, as well as entry-level positions, leading to a K-shaped labor market where demand for experienced, high-skilled AI professionals rises while opportunities for freshers shrink. This creates significant anxiety among the workforce, with a survey revealing that 66% of AI/ML workers in India anticipate layoffs or staffing reductions within 3-6 months.

The rapid pace of AI adoption also exacerbates the existing skill gap within the Indian workforce. Many employees lack the necessary skills to transition into new AI-related roles, and reskilling efforts are not keeping pace with the rate of job displacement. This mismatch leaves a significant portion of the workforce vulnerable and can deepen economic inequality, as a small, highly skilled elite benefits while others struggle to find new employment.

Moreover, mass layoffs can have a cascading negative impact on the broader economy. Reduced consumer spending, particularly from the urban middle class, can slow down various sectors like real estate, automotive, and retail, which are heavily reliant on the tech workforce's purchasing power. This collective reduction in demand, where companies acting in self-interest simultaneously cut jobs, can ironically weaken the overall economic environment, even for the companies that automated first.

Key facts

  • Globally, over 6,300 tech jobs were cut in the first 10 days of September 2026, with Indian operations of companies like Uber and PayPal affected, partly due to AI focus.
  • More than 175,000 technology workers globally lost jobs in 2026 so far, as companies restructure, cut costs, and shift towards AI and other new technologies.
  • While AI creates demand for high-skill roles like AI engineers and data analysts, it is reducing or replacing middle and low-skilled jobs, particularly impacting entry-level hiring in India.

The bottom line

The debate over AI-driven layoffs in Indian tech companies underscores a complex challenge for the nation. While AI promises increased efficiency and innovation, driving economic growth and global competitiveness, its implementation raises critical questions about job security, workforce preparedness, and broader societal impact. As India navigates this technological shift, balancing corporate imperatives with social responsibility remains a central concern for the future of its vast tech workforce.
