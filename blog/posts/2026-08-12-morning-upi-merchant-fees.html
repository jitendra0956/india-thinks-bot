India Debates UPI Merchant Fees: A Shift in Digital Payment Policy?

August 12, 2026 · 3 min read

India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) has been a global success story, revolutionizing digital transactions with its ease of use and, crucially, its zero-merchant-discount-rate (MDR) policy since January 2020. This policy meant businesses faced no charges for accepting UPI or RuPay debit card payments, significantly boosting digital adoption across the country. However, a pivotal shift occurred with the introduction and passage of The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, on August 4, 2026. This legislation has removed the statutory prohibition on levying merchant fees for UPI and RuPay debit card transactions, granting the Central Government the power to introduce charges through future notifications. While the Finance Minister has clarified that consumers will continue to enjoy free UPI transactions, this development has ignited a nationwide debate: should the ban on UPI merchant fees have been removed, or should the zero-MDR regime have remained?

Why people say yes

Advocates for allowing UPI merchant fees argue that the zero-MDR policy, while successful in driving adoption, has made the digital payments ecosystem unsustainable for banks and payment service providers. These entities incur significant costs related to maintaining infrastructure, developing new technologies, and ensuring robust security against fraud. Introducing a fee, particularly for high-value transactions or larger merchants, can provide a much-needed revenue stream to cover these operational expenses and fund future investments in the UPI network. This financial injection is seen as crucial for fostering innovation, upgrading security features, and expanding UPI's reach and services both domestically and internationally. Proponents believe this move would ensure the long-term sustainability and growth of India's leading digital payment system, allowing it to evolve without relying solely on government subsidies.

Why people say no

Opponents of removing the merchant fee ban express concerns about its potential negative impact on small businesses and overall digital payment adoption. They argue that even a small fee could significantly increase operating costs for merchants, especially smaller enterprises like Kirana stores, who often operate on thin margins. This added financial burden might deter these businesses from continuing to accept UPI payments, or from adopting them in the first place, thereby hindering the progress of digital inclusion. While the government has assured that consumers will not be directly charged for UPI transactions, there is a strong concern that merchants might pass on their increased costs to consumers through higher prices for goods and services. Critics fear that this indirect cost burden could make digital payments less attractive, potentially leading some users to revert to cash, undermining the strides made towards a less-cash economy.

Key facts

  • The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, passed by Parliament, enables the Central Government to notify which electronic payment modes may attract charges, removing the previous statutory prohibition on UPI merchant fees.
  • The zero-Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) policy for UPI and RuPay debit card transactions was implemented in January 2020 to accelerate the adoption of digital payments in India.
  • Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has clarified that consumers will not face any transaction charges for UPI, and any potential fees would apply to merchants, likely for high-value transactions or large businesses.

The bottom line

The parliamentary decision to remove the statutory ban on UPI merchant fees marks a significant moment for India's digital payments landscape. This change creates a legal framework that prioritizes the long-term financial health and technological advancement of the UPI ecosystem by allowing for a potential revenue stream. However, it also introduces a critical challenge: ensuring that such a policy shift does not inadvertently burden small businesses or impede the widespread digital adoption that UPI has so successfully achieved. The ongoing debate reflects the complex task of balancing innovation, sustainability, and financial inclusion within India's rapidly evolving digital economy.
