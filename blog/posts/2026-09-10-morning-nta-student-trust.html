India's Exam Conundrum: Can the NTA Rebuild Student Trust?

September 10, 2026 · 3 min read

India's National Testing Agency (NTA), established to standardize and streamline high-stakes examinations, is currently facing a significant crisis of confidence. Recent reports on September 8, 2026, highlighted years of recurrent paper leaks and pervasive administrative errors that have severely undermined student and public trust in the country's crucial examination system. This escalating challenge has led to widespread student protests and calls for substantial reforms, casting a shadow over the integrity of national entrance tests that determine the academic and professional futures of millions.

Why people say yes

Many argue that the NTA has undeniably lost student trust due to a pattern of systemic failures. Numerous instances of paper leaks in high-profile examinations like NEET-UG and UGC-NET have repeatedly forced re-examinations, causing immense stress and uncertainty for students nationwide. Beyond leaks, the agency has been plagued by administrative errors, including technical glitches, discrepancies in grace marks, faulty scoring, delayed results, and issues at exam centers, further eroding faith in its operational competence. Critics also point to a perceived lack of transparency and accountability in NTA's functioning and its investigations into malpractices, leading to a general sentiment that outcomes are not always merit-based. The continuous cycle of errors, protests, and subsequent governmental interventions underscores a deep-seated institutional problem that has left students feeling vulnerable and disillusioned.

Why people say no

Conversely, some believe that despite the setbacks, the NTA still retains a degree of student trust, or at least remains a necessary component of India's examination landscape. The sheer scale of examinations conducted by the NTA is colossal, managing tests for over 1 crore (10 million) candidates annually across numerous high-stakes exams like JEE Main, NEET-UG, and CUET. Given this vast scope, minor issues are, to some extent, anticipated. Crucially, the NTA and the government have consistently taken corrective actions in response to controversies, such as re-conducting examinations, removing hundreds of experts, overhauling question paper scrutiny systems, and implementing enhanced security measures like CISF deployment at offices. Furthermore, a centralized testing body like NTA is deemed essential for maintaining uniform standards and efficiency across India's diverse education system, and currently, there is no immediately viable alternative to conduct national-level entrance examinations. Ongoing reforms, including a high-level task force and new legislative measures like the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, demonstrate a commitment to strengthening the system rather than abandoning it entirely.

Key facts

  • The NTA was established in 2017 to bring efficiency, transparency, and standardization to India's entrance examinations.
  • In 2026 alone, NEET-UG and UGC-NET examinations faced paper leak allegations and administrative errors, leading to re-examinations for millions of students.
  • The government has initiated reforms including a four-tier question paper checking system, the removal of approximately 600 experts, and plans for CISF-secured NTA offices to enhance security and integrity.

The bottom line

The NTA stands at a critical juncture, with its credibility questioned by recurring controversies. While the scale of its operations and the ongoing governmental reforms aim to restore faith, the persistent issues of paper leaks and administrative lapses have deeply impacted student morale and aspirations. The path forward for NTA involves not just implementing stricter security and technological solutions but also fostering a renewed sense of transparency, accountability, and reliability to truly rebuild the trust of India's student community in its vital examination system.
