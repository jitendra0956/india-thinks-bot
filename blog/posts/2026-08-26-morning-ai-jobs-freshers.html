AI's Impact: Are India's Freshers **Losing Out**?

August 26, 2026 · 3 min read

A recent Nomura report, released on August 23, 2026, has ignited a crucial debate about the evolving landscape of India's job market. While the report indicates a net positive in overall job creation due to Artificial Intelligence (AI), it highlights a significant concern: freshers are disproportionately affected by reduced entry-level hiring. This phenomenon, described as a 'K-shaped' job market, suggests that AI's benefits are not evenly distributed, creating both opportunities and considerable challenges for India's young workforce. Understanding this dynamic is vital as India positions itself as a global AI hub.

Why people say yes

Proponents arguing that AI disproportionately harms freshers point to the observed decline in entry-level recruitment across various sectors. Companies are increasingly automating routine tasks that were traditionally performed by junior employees, leading to fewer openings for new graduates. For instance, a survey of 651 Indian IT companies revealed that 55% had scaled back entry-level hiring. This shift means that the initial rungs of the career ladder are becoming increasingly sparse, making it harder for freshers to gain foundational experience.

Furthermore, the nature of jobs created by AI often requires specialized skills that many fresh graduates currently lack. The rapid technological advancements mean that university curricula may not always keep pace with industry demands, resulting in a significant skills mismatch. Reports indicate that a substantial percentage of Indian organizations identify AI, digital, and data skills as major workforce constraints, exacerbating the difficulty for freshers to secure relevant employment.

The automation of basic and repetitive tasks by AI tools reduces the need for large entry-level teams. Tasks like boilerplate coding, testing, and documentation, once learning grounds for freshers, are now increasingly handled by AI. This not only limits job availability but also diminishes opportunities for new hires to acquire on-the-job experience and tacit knowledge crucial for career progression, potentially creating a vacuum in future leadership pipelines.

Why people say no

Conversely, those who believe AI does not disproportionately harm freshers, or even benefits the job market overall, emphasize the creation of new, high-value job roles. While some entry-level tasks may be automated, AI fosters demand for data scientists, AI engineers, machine learning specialists, and prompt engineers, among others. The Nomura report itself noted that AI-related hiring in India recorded 83,100 new jobs against 31,921 positions lost between 2022 and August 2026, indicating a net positive impact.

The need for upskilling and reskilling is seen not as a barrier but as an opportunity for freshers to adapt and thrive. Educational institutions and training programs are evolving to equip students with AI-centric skills, enabling them to pursue these emerging roles. With tailored training, freshers can transition into positions that involve managing, developing, or integrating AI systems, thus opening up new career pathways that were previously non-existent.

Finally, the broader economic impact of AI is generally positive, leading to increased productivity, innovation, and overall economic growth. This expansion can ultimately create more jobs across various sectors, even if the nature of these jobs changes. India's large workforce and its status as a global IT services hub mean that while disruption is evident, the potential for AI to drive significant economic value and employment growth remains substantial, benefiting the nation's talent pool in the long run.

Key facts

  • A Nomura report from August 2026 found that AI generated approximately 131,000 AI-related jobs across Asia between 2022 and August 2026, against 61,000 jobs lost, with India showing a net positive impact.
  • The same report indicated a "K-shaped" labour market in India, where entry-level hiring is declining while demand for experienced professionals with deeper business knowledge is rising.
  • A survey of 651 Indian IT companies found that 55% had reduced entry-level hiring, significantly higher than the 14% reporting a decline in senior positions.

The bottom line

The impact of AI on India's job market is a multifaceted issue, presenting both challenges and opportunities. While there is clear evidence of reduced entry-level hiring affecting freshers, AI is also driving the creation of new, specialized roles and fostering overall economic growth. The ongoing debate highlights the critical need for a workforce that can adapt to technological shifts through continuous learning and skill development. Ultimately, navigating this transformation successfully will require concerted efforts from individuals, educational institutions, and industries to ensure that India's diverse talent pool remains competitive and benefits from the AI revolution.
