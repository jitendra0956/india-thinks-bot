Tech Layoffs in India: Market Correction or Unjustified Cuts?

September 01, 2026 · 3 min read

India's tech sector is currently navigating a period of significant change, marked by reported job cuts at major firms like Oracle and performance improvement plans (PIPs) at Microsoft. This wave of workforce restructuring, amidst a booming yet evolving digital economy, has sparked a nationwide debate: are these layoffs a necessary market correction driven by technological advancements and shifting demands, or do they represent unjustified cuts impacting a vital workforce?

Why people say yes

Many argue that the current tech layoffs are a necessary response to a rapidly changing market. The advent of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and automation is fundamentally reshaping job roles, leading to the redundancy of traditional, routine functions. Companies are restructuring to prioritize efficiency and invest heavily in new technologies like AI and cloud computing, making some existing skill sets obsolete. The Nifty IT index, a key indicator of the sector's health, has significantly underperformed the broader market in 2025 and 2026, reflecting underlying challenges and a need for recalibration. Furthermore, some cuts are seen as a correction to the aggressive over-hiring witnessed during the pandemic years. The global economic slowdown and geopolitical uncertainties have also contributed to reduced discretionary spending from international clients, impacting revenue growth and necessitating cost optimization measures. This perspective views the layoffs as a strategic move to align the workforce with future industry demands and ensure long-term competitiveness.

Why people say no

Conversely, many contend that the current wave of tech layoffs is not entirely justified. They point to the robust overall growth in India's IT spending, projected to exceed $176 billion in 2026, with double-digit growth expected in IT services. This sustained investment, particularly in cloud and digital technologies, suggests a healthy and expanding market, not one in decline. Companies like Microsoft are making substantial multi-billion dollar commitments to expand AI infrastructure and workforce development in India, indicating long-term confidence in the sector. Critics also argue that many layoffs stem from a skill mismatch rather than a genuine lack of demand; while some roles become redundant, there's a simultaneous surge in demand for AI engineers and data scientists. The prevalence of "silent layoffs" through performance-linked exits, rather than transparent restructuring, raises concerns about fairness and employee well-being. Moreover, India's domestic demand remains resilient, and AI is also seen as a significant opportunity to create new, high-value jobs, rather than solely a threat.

Key facts

  • India's IT spending is forecast to reach over $176 billion in 2026, representing a 10.6% increase from 2025, driven by cloud and digital technologies.
  • Estimates suggest India's technology and software services industry could see 25,000-35,000 job eliminations in 2026, with some reports indicating cumulative job losses across 2025 and 2026 could reach up to 43,000.
  • AI adoption is a primary driver behind current workforce restructuring, with companies prioritizing efficiency and automating routine tasks, even while reporting strong revenues in some cases.

The bottom line

The debate surrounding tech layoffs in India reflects the complex interplay of rapid technological advancement, global economic shifts, and evolving business strategies. While the industry is undoubtedly undergoing a transformation driven by AI and a focus on efficiency, there are differing perspectives on whether the scale and nature of current job reductions are fully warranted. The long-term outlook for India's tech sector remains dynamic, with both significant challenges and opportunities on the horizon.
